POST 1

I am building today in the Product Space Agentic AI Hackathon.
We have one day to build from scratch and submit by midnight.

There were five problem statements to pick from.
I picked the Career pivot coach.

Most career tools stop at advice.
They give you a generic list and tell you to "learn SQL".
They never look at what companies are asking for this week.
They never check if your applications actually got a reply.

My plan is a coach that closes the loop:
It reads real job postings live.
It maps them to your resume using exact lines as proof.
It builds a week by week plan.
It runs mock interviews scored on a rubric.
Then it learns from which applications get replies.

It is called Tack.
The name comes from sailing: changing tack means a deliberate change of course.

I will share progress later today.

POST 2

Quick progress update on Tack, my career pivot coach for the Product Space Agentic AI Hackathon.

What works right now:
It pulled 40 real AI Product Manager postings from 60 company career boards in about 10 seconds.
That includes Razorpay, Glean, Paytm, Sarvam AI, UiPath, and others.

The gap map:
It maps every skill those postings ask for.
Each skill is sized by how often it comes up.
It is filled by how much proof your resume shows.
Click any skill to see the resume line on the left and the posting lines on the right.

Mock interviews:
Each answer is scored on five criteria.
Every score quotes your own words back to you.

One thing I learned:
The model is good at reading and judging, but I do not let it do the maths.
Demand, readiness, and the plan schedule are plain code.
That way, every number can be explained.
Every quote is also checked to be a real substring of the source, so nothing is made up.

POST 3 (final post, with the demo video attached)

I built Tack in one day for the Product Space Agentic AI Hackathon.
The problem statement was to build a career pivot coach.

Most career tools stop at giving advice.
Tack actually closes the loop.

Here is what it does:

It checks the market live.
It scanned 10,654 roles on 60 real company boards.
It read 40 AI Product Manager postings in about 11 seconds.

It maps skill gaps with proof.
Every skill points to the exact line in your resume and the exact lines in the job post, with company names.

It plans your career move week by week.
If you fall behind, it replans and explains what moved and why.

It runs mock interviews scored on five criteria.
Every score quotes your own words back to you.

It applies for you in a real browser.
It can run several job applications at the same time.

It reads replies and learns what types of roles answer you.

Autopilot runs that whole loop on a schedule.

How I built it:

The browser engine is Fanfold, a project I built earlier.
Everything else was built today.

The model reads and judges.
Code handles the maths.
Every quote is checked against its source, or dropped.

It has 176 automated tests.
It works as a desktop app, a web version, and an MCP server.

What I was careful about:
I did not send fake applications to real companies.
The demo applies to a practice form.
On real Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby forms, I ran dry runs that fill everything out and stop before submit.

My takeaway:
An agent is only useful when you can check its work.
Quotes, numbers from code, and a visible trace made the difference.
